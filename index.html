CHAPTER 02 • CASE FILE

A CASE WITHOUT A FINAL LOCATION

At 00:41 MYT on 8 March 2014, Flight MH370 took off from Kuala Lumpur International Airport bound for Beijing Capital. It had 239 people on board and fuel endurance for 7 hours and 31 minutes. Thirty-eight minutes into the flight, it vanished from civilian radar screens.

FLIGHT CREW 12 MALAYSIAN CITIZENS Capt. Zaharie (53) • F/O Fariq (27)
CRUISING LEVEL FL350 (35,000 FT) Airspeed ~470 knots (870 km/h)
FUEL ON BOARD 49,100 KG Endurance ~7 hr 31 min

120,000 km² underwater sonar search along 7th Arc completed without locating fuselage or engines.

Underwater acoustic locator beacons (37.5 kHz) expired in April 2014 without acoustic contact.

PHYSICAL DEBRIS • CONFIRMED RECOVERED

Over 30 pieces found in Western Indian Ocean; 3 confirmed 9M-MRO components (Flaperon, Stabilizer, Outboard Flap).

Malaysian MOT Safety Investigation Final Report concludes team was unable to determine the cause of the diversion.

CHAPTER 05 • DEFENSE SURVEILLANCE

THE RADAR TRACK DID NOT END. THE STORY DID.

When civilian air traffic controllers lost the aircraft, air defense radar systems continued to record its physical skin reflections.

RMAF BUTTERWORTH / GONG KEDAK PSR 12 RPM • 2.8 GHZ
CIVILIAN SECONDARY SURVEILLANCE RADAR (SSR)

COOPERATIVE TRANSPONDER INTERROGATION

Relies on the onboard transponder to return Squawk 2157, barometric altitude (FL350), and GPS coordinates. Ceased at 01:21:13 MYT at waypoint IGARI. No further secondary radar contacts occurred.

STATUS: CEASED AT FIR BOUNDARY
MILITARY PRIMARY SURVEILLANCE RADAR (PSR)

NON-COOPERATIVE SKIN REFLECTION

Measures electromagnetic pulse echos off the titanium and aluminum skin of the fuselage. Tracked the aircraft making an unscheduled turn across Peninsular Malaysia to Penang, Pulau Perak, and waypoint MEKAR.

STATUS: ACTIVE UNTIL 02:22 MYT (MEKAR)
OFFICIAL AIR DEFENSE RECORD: The Royal Malaysian Air Force stated the target was identified as a non-hostile friendly commercial aircraft, which is why scramble fighters were not dispatched from Butterworth Air Base.
CHAPTER 06 • AIRSPACE DEAD ZONE

THE TURN

The deviation occurred in the exact gap between Malaysian and Vietnamese air traffic control. The aircraft did not veer randomly; it navigated through recognized aeronautical waypoints.

DOCUMENTED FACT

Tactical Airspace Timing

The turn was initiated within seconds of handoff between Malaysian ATC and Vietnamese ATC, during which neither controller held active surveillance responsibility.

DOCUMENTED FACT

Navigational Waypoint Tracking

The aircraft navigated along airway N571 toward waypoints VAMPI and MEKAR, which required intentional manual flying or programmed Flight Management Computer (FMC) commands.

INVESTIGATIVE INFERENCE

Human Intervention

The Malaysian Safety Investigation Report concluded: "The change in flight path was likely made under manual control or programmed autopilot input by someone with flight proficiency."

UNKNOWN

Cockpit Control State

It remains unverified who was seated in the command seats, whether cabin depressurization occurred, or if unlawful interference took place.

CHAPTER 07 • STRATOSPHERIC SIGNALS

THE SEVENTH ARC: INMARSAT HANDSHAKES

Seven hours after radar loss, the aircraft’s Satellite Data Unit (SDU) transmitted automated hourly radio "handshakes" to an Inmarsat-3 F1 geostationary satellite 35,786 kilometers above the equator.

1st Arc (02:25) 2nd Arc (03:41) 3rd Arc (04:41) 4th Arc (05:41) 5th Arc (06:41) 6th Arc (08:11) 7th Arc (08:19 Fuel Flameout)
FIGURE 7.1: Burst Timing Offset (BTO) distance rings from Inmarsat-3 F1 (64.5°E) intersecting the Earth's surface in the Southern Indian Ocean.

TWO SCIENTIFIC MEASUREMENTS

BURST TIMING OFFSET (BTO)

Measures the microsecond time delay between Perth ground station, satellite, and aircraft. Defined concentric distance rings on Earth at each hour.

BURST FREQUENCY OFFSET (BFO)

Measures Doppler shifts caused by aircraft velocity relative to the satellite. Ruled out the Northern Corridor (Central Asia) and proved flight into the Southern Indian Ocean.

CHAPTER 09 • PHYSICAL EVIDENCE

THE PIECES THAT SURFACED

Physical evidence proves the aircraft entered the Indian Ocean. Over thirty marine debris fragments washed ashore along the coasts of East Africa, Madagascar, and Indian Ocean islands.

CONFIRMED 9M-MRO
EV-001 • 29 JULY 2015

Right-Wing Flaperon (Réunion Island)

Found on a beach in Saint-André, Réunion. Internal serial numbers (657BB) confirmed it belonged to 9M-MRO. French DGA / BEA examination concluded trailing-edge damage was consistent with high-energy impact, not a controlled low-speed ditching.

Saint-André, Réunion Island • [SRC-006]
ALMOST CERTAINLY 9M-MRO
EV-002 • DEC 2015 / MAR 2016

Horizontal Stabilizer Fairing ("NO STEP")

Found on a sandbank in Mozambique by Blaine Gibson. Stenciled font and color matched Boeing specifications used exclusively by Malaysia Airlines. ATSB concluded it almost certainly originated from 9M-MRO.

Vilankulo, Mozambique • [SRC-002]
CONFIRMED 9M-MRO
EV-003 • 24 JUNE 2016

Right Outboard Flap (Pemba, Tanzania)

Large section of right wing flap. Manufacturer part stamps verified origin from 9M-MRO. Critical finding: the flap was retracted inside the wing when it struck the water, contradicting controlled ditching theories.

Pemba Island, Tanzania • [SRC-002]
CSIRO & GEOMAR REVERSE-DRIFT MODELING: Oceanographic simulations tracking the South Equatorial Current across 16–24 months corroborated that debris recovered in East Africa originated from the 7th Arc between latitudes 32°S and 36°S.

WHAT WE KNOW

ESTABLISHED FACTS
  • Takeoff from KLIA at 00:41 MYT, normal climb to FL350.
  • Final voice contact at 01:19:30 MYT: "Good night Malaysian three seven zero."
  • Mode-S transponder ceased at 01:21:13 MYT at waypoint IGARI.
  • Primary air defense radar tracked a 110° turn across Peninsular Malaysia to MEKAR.
  • Inmarsat SDU logged 7 hourly handshakes, confirming flight until 08:19 MYT.
  • Over 30 debris fragments recovered; flaperon and flap confirmed 9M-MRO.
  • Right outboard flap was retracted inside wing at water impact.

WHAT WAS INFERRED

SCIENTIFIC DEDUCTION
  • Turn followed standard aeronautical waypoints (VAMPI, MEKAR), requiring human FMC or manual inputs.
  • Doppler BFO shifts mathematically prove a southward flight path terminating on the 7th Arc.
  • The 08:19:37 unprompted log-on was triggered by electrical reboot following fuel starvation.
  • Final high descent rate (>15,000 fpm) indicates an unpiloted steep spiraling dive.
  • Reverse-drift oceanographic models converge on Southern Indian Ocean origin.

WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN

UNRESOLVED MYSTERIES
  • Who was at the flight controls when the transponder was disabled.
  • The motivation behind the unscheduled flight path diversion.
  • Cockpit state: whether hypoxia, unlawful interference, or crew incapacitation occurred.
  • Exact resting coordinates of the main fuselage on the ocean floor.
  • The physical flight recorders (CVR & FDR) remain unfound.
